\begin{abstract}
A numerically correct answer can rely on a measurement that does not justify the scientific claim. In atomistic evidence, identical units can describe different atom sets, coordinate frames, endpoint comparisons, or alignment conventions. We investigate this distinction with GeoScope, a definition-aware benchmark and finite scientific proof checker. In the original 55-source-group study (1,760 claim variants), full-definition checking rejected 81 Qwen acceptances that comparison-identity checking allowed, while retaining 312 licensed numerical answers; these covered only 28.36\% of 1,100 answerable questions. We then evaluated four open-weight models on 48 independently sourced Open Catalyst 2020 trajectories (1,536 questions per model). Among 960 answerable external questions, fence-normalized valid-proof coverage ranged from 10.1\% for Phi-4-mini to 59.1\% for Qwen3-32B; the latter still produced 393 invalid proofs. No model gave an exact scientifically justified abstention on the 576 insufficient-evidence external questions. Replayed token probabilities matched all 6,144 original model outputs. Decision-token confidence discriminated correct from incorrect proofs with area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) between 0.552 and 0.724, yet 185 of the 32B model's 601 answers with decision-token score above 0.99 were invalid. Deterministic enumeration resolves the controlled task when its declared rules and structured evidence are supplied, so we do not claim that an LLM--verifier hybrid exceeds the rule-only oracle. The results distinguish numerical plausibility, licensed physical evidence, model confidence, and scientific sufficiency.
\end{abstract}
