\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

GeoScope isolates a scientific failure that cannot be detected by checking arithmetic or final-answer labels alone: a genuine measurement can use the wrong atoms, endpoint comparison, coordinate frame, alignment, or physical relation. In the original 55-group study, full physical-definition checking rejected 81 additional invalid Qwen acceptances without discarding any of the 312 licensed accepted numerical answers, but only 28.36\% of answerable cases retained a licensed response. The failure mechanisms persisted across four open-weight language models on 48 independently sourced OC20 atomic systems. Qwen3-32B led the fence-normalized external comparison with 59.1\% licensed proofs; even this model produced 393 invalid proofs on 960 answerable questions. Decision-token confidence was useful for ranking answers (32B AUROC 0.724) but could remain very high on invalid proofs, and none of the models reliably abstained under the frozen contract. These findings support treating physical definitions, evidence sufficiency, and confidence as separate evaluation dimensions. They motivate independent physics-aware checking but do not demonstrate superiority over a deterministic-only enumerator, a general-purpose fact checker, or validated deployment-level abstention.
